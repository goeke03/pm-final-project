- **Moment of misery, what is the user likely doing outside the product (Google, spreadsheets, a competitor) because your tool isn’t serving them?:** When the plan doesn't fit their month, the user likely leaves the product to:

Google it: "what happens if I miss a payment", "is this link legitimate". They need reassurance and the consequences in plain language.
Work it out in a spreadsheet or notes app: they list income, bills and payday to see what they can afford. The product's default plan doesn't use that real cash flow.
Check their banking app: they want to know the payment won't bounce.
Look at alternatives: a competitor's pay-later tool, a debt charity, or a loan or card to cover the balance.
Hunt for a phone number: their case is non-standard and they can't find a visible route to a person.
Ask friends, family or forums: "should I agree to this?"
Do nothing: they close the tab out of shame or fear.
- **Problem hook, summarize the business risk and user pain into one urgent sentence.:** Consumers in financial pressure are being pushed into instalment plans they can't keep, or leaving to work it out elsewhere, so creditors lose recoveries, agents inherit cold, context-free cases, and the product's promise of a plan that holds erodes with every missed first payment.
- **Value proposition, based on the initiative in your assigned Riverty case, what is the new value and why is it urgent to launch now?:** New value: CFor consumers who can't pay a balance in full, we build the plan around your real income and payday, tell you plainly what happens next, and if your case needs a person, they already know your story.

Why launch now:

Consumers expect phone-free resolution, and delay makes balances harder to recover.
Budget pressure raises demand for plans people can keep.
Expectations on affordability and vulnerable-customer handling are rising.
Competitors can move first on better affordability checks and warm handoffs.
Plans agreed on a guess break, and the plan-kept rate only improves with live data.
The quick wins are cheap: context-rich handoff, payday-aligned dates and reminders.
One-line proposition: Help consumers agree a payment plan they can actually keep, on their own time, and hand them to an agent with full context when their case needs a person.

## Cold-read your own hook
- **Is the business risk high enough to justify a new initiative, a high-stakes threat, or a minor inconvenience?:** the risk justifies a new, scoped initiative.
My recommendation is to fund a discovery sprint now, not a full build. Measure plan-kept rate at 30, 60 and 90 days, and ship the low-effort items in parallel. After the sprint, escalate if two or more threat signals are confirmed.
- **Is the moment of misery a systemic problem or just an edge case?:** Verdict: the moment of misery is mostly systemic.

Systemic: the trigger is the plan-selection step, which every user passes through. The exit behaviours (Googling, spreadsheets, checking the banking app, hunting for a phone number) are common patterns for people who owe money. Several causes overlap, and the target group is already under financial pressure. Exits also feed themselves, because plans agreed on a guess break and reach agents with no context.
True edge cases: disputed or legally escalated claims, hardship or vulnerability, failed identity checks, and very low digital confidence. These are a minority, and they are the reason the handoff exists.
