# Competitive Analysis & Journey Map (Module 2)

## Responses
- **Role, who are you solving for? (the specific user segment or profile):** The consumer arranging payment under pressure
- **Goal, what is this user ultimately trying to achieve?:** To commit only to an instalment they can pay, or to get a clear answer on cost before they agree.
- **Friction, the main barrier (moment of misery) stopping them from succeeding:** On the plan list, the option with the fewest months looks like the right answer. They take it, the amount is too high, and the second payment is missed. When that confidence is missing, they leave the screen instead: the income question is abandoned on a break, the decision moves to a phone call about cost, or they refuse to agree until the portal matches the letter.
- **External tools, the outside platforms or tools the user is forced to use:** no external tool
- **The process, the 3 to 5 manual steps the user takes to get the job done:** The process. The 3 to 5 manual steps this consumer takes to get the job done:

Open the portal and attempt the income question and the plan list, often in a short break.
Set the letter against the amount on screen and check whether the two figures match.
Leave without agreeing when the income period, the cost, or the two amounts are still unclear. They close the journey, or they refuse to accept the plan.
Call a person and ask whether the arrangement will cost more, and which amount is the one to accept.
Give that person the same details already typed online, then agree only after the explanation.
- **Core frustration, the exact moment the process feels most “broken”:** They are on the call they made to learn the cost before agreeing, with the letter in front of them because it does not match the screen, and the agent asks for the same details again and still cannot say which amount is right or why that plan was offered.
- **The evidence, a specific quote or behavior from the research that proves this:** The evidence. A specific quote or behavior from the research that proves this:

"Honestly I prefer to call. A person can tell me if this is going to cost me more."
"The amount online was not the amount in the letter. I did not want to agree to anything until someone explained it."
"Half my calls start with me asking for the same details they already typed online. I cannot see what they entered."
"When a plan fails I need to know why it was offered in the first place. Right now I just see a number."

- **Your journey map file name (e.g. journey-map.html):** Future-state journey
The consumer arranging payment under pressure. Four stages, from payday to a person who already knows the story.

Value proposition
For consumers who can’t pay a balance in full, we build the plan around your real income and payday, tell you plainly what happens next, and if your case needs a person, they already know your story.
1
Real income and payday

User action

Enter this payday’s income -> The plan is built from what they earn.

Internal state

The period and purpose are clear -> They can answer during the break.

Pain point addressed

Income question closed on a break -> They answer a named payday and continue.

2
What happens next

User action

Review instalment, missed payment, and the letter amount -> They judge the cost on screen.

Internal state

The cost and the amount make sense -> They can judge the plan before agreeing.

Pain point addressed

Portal amount differed from the letter -> They see one explained figure.

3
Agree on screen

User action

Confirm the payday-sized instalment -> They commit only to what they can pay.

Internal state

This instalment looks payable -> They expect to keep the second payment.

Pain point addressed

Fewest months looked right, then broke -> The term is sized before agreement.

4
A known story

User action

Reach a person only when still needed -> That person already has their story.

Internal state

A person already knows their story -> They can ask for help without starting over.

Pain point addressed

Calls restarted details already typed -> The agent explains without a second intake.

Competitive advantages
Against the manual workaround: close and return, check the letter, call, and repeat the same details.

Payday builds the term -> They agree an instalment they can keep.

2

Next step is plain on screen -> They decide without leaving to call.

3
