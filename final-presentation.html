Slide 5 · Strategy

This bet is two sentences. It is not the 6.5-point gap.

Problem statement hook

Consumers in financial pressure are being pushed into instalment plans they can't keep, or leaving to work it out elsewhere, so creditors lose recoveries, agents inherit cold, context-free cases, and the product's promise of a plan that holds erodes with every missed first payment.

Value proposition · this release

For a consumer in shift or variable work who cannot pay in full and is on a short break, the income field asks for this month, says the number sizes the instalment, and says the balance owed does not change.

The journey line, a plan built on payday with a handoff that already knows the story, stays on the roadmap. It is not what this copy ships. Persona: the shift-income consumer.

Hypothesis this test can carry

I believe that the field label “This month’s income” plus the two sentences on why it is asked and how it is used, for that shift-work consumer, will produce more finished agreements, as measured by a 2 percentage-point rise in the variable-or-shift agreement rate within 9 months. The 60-day no-miss hold for this segment stays at or above 77.5% and at or above control.

Context, not the claim: fixed salary agrees at 48.1%. That 6.5-point gap is the wider income problem. This copy does not say how long the number is kept, and it does not claim the gap.

Slide 6 · Research

They leave the screen. The screen has no workaround.

Competitive analysis / workaround

The sample names no external software, calculator, or budgeting tool for this persona. The only bypasses it records are manual, and each one takes the decision off the plan screen. The person who stays on that screen has no workaround at all: the fewest months looks like the right answer, they take it, and the second payment is missed.

  • Close the journey and mean to come back. The income question does not say whether the figure is this month or a normal month.
  • Call a person. N-04: “Honestly I prefer to call. A person can tell me if this is going to cost me more.” why_income_is_asked is 419 of 2,592 agent contacts (16.2%), average 11.8 minutes.
  • Trust the letter. N-07: “The amount online was not the amount in the letter. I did not want to agree to anything until someone explained it.”

Full journey · not this release

  • Payday builds the term. Separate card. Not in this test.
  • The next step is plain on screen. This bet only does that on the income question.
  • The story stays on the case. Agent handoff is Next, not Now.

MoSCoW · the plain income explainer

Must

On the income screen, above the field, readable on a phone. Label: this month’s income. Two sentences always visible. One action finishes the step.

Should

One clause for a thin month. A pass-through count for variable-or-shift. A retention sentence only if already approved.

Could

Repeat the line on plan review. An inline privacy link. A hint under the field, with no example amount.

Won’t now

A second income field. A change to the calculation. Photo upload. Agent-screen copy, chat, or a help-centre page as the home of the explanation.

PRD highlights

Vision. On a short break, a shift-work consumer sees that we want this month’s income to size an instalment, and can enter it without fearing the number will be used against them.

Screen B copy, always visible: “We ask for this month’s income so the instalment can fit the month you are in.” “This number sizes that instalment. It does not change the balance you owe.”

Screens: A entry, “Set up a plan for the balance you cannot pay in full.” · B income, label “This month’s income.” · C confirmation, “This month’s income is saved.” One HTML file, plain JavaScript, no external APIs, no login.

Slide 8 · Validation

One change. One read at month 9.

Hypothesis

I believe that the plain explainer, the field label “This month’s income” plus the two sentences on why it is asked and how it is used, for a consumer in shift or variable work who cannot pay the balance in full and is setting up an instalment plan on a short break, will result in more of them finishing an agreement because they can place the figure as this month’s income and continue, as measured by a 2 percentage-point change in the agreement rate for variable-or-shift starters within 9 months from the start. We will protect the share of this segment’s new agreements with no missed payment within 60 days throughout the test.

Control

The current income screen. Plan order, chat, the document step, and the agent desktop stay as they are.

Variant

The only change is the income label “This month’s income” and the two sentences on the current income screen.

Primary metric

Agreement rate for variable-or-shift starters.

Baseline 41.6% on 2,862 starters. MDE ≥ 2 percentage points, two-sided p < 0.05. About 9,600 per arm at 80% power. At a flat 77.5% hold that is about 22 extra kept agreements a month on the treatment arm, about 155 across the 7 intake months, and about 44 a month if the full segment ships.

Guardrail

60-day no-miss hold for this segment. Floor 77.5%, and not below the control arm. Overall floor on the wider book is 81.0%.

Intake and read

First 7 sample-months. About 1,430 starters per arm in a sample month. The single read is 60 days after intake closes. The read date is fixed at the end of those 9 months. No results are reviewed before this date. A 14-day peek cannot see a 2-point lift or a 60-day miss.

Ship

We will SHIP if the agreement rate for variable-or-shift starters improves by ≥ 2 percentage points at two-sided p < 0.05 and the 60-day no-miss hold for this segment does not fall below 77.5%, and does not fall below the control arm, after 9 months from the start.

Iterate

We will ITERATE if the agreement rate is higher than control, the lift is below 2 percentage points, and the hold is still at or above 77.5% and at or above control.

Kill

We will KILL if the agreement rate shows no improvement or moves negatively, or if the hold falls below 77.5% or below control.

These figures are the fictional case sample, not Riverty results.

Slide 10 · Story

Kept agreements are the bet. More broken plans are not.

Aha

This segment does not need a larger book of plans. Of 2,862 starters, 41.6% agree and 22.5% of those agreements miss inside 60 days, the highest miss rate in the file. An agreement that misses is not reliable money.

Two sentences can be tested for a 2-point move. They do not close the 6.5-point gap to a fixed salary, they do not size the plan to payday, and they do not stop the agent from asking for the same details again. N-01 is the friction this copy touches. N-06, T-206, and N-08 are why the words have to be on the screen, not in a call.

What I would do next

  • Fund the copy test. Keep every external channel dark until the single read at month 9.
  • Count kept agreements, not agreements. About 22 extra a month in the test, about 44 a month if the segment ships, hold flat at 77.5%.
  • The funded bet stays the explainer. Payday date stays on its own card. Felix owns the help page. Florian is the marketing contact, and only after a ship call.
  • Ship only at ≥ 2 points and a hold at or above 77.5% and at or above control. Under that, rewrite or kill before any announcement.
  • Do not fund paid media on this read. A rollout past this segment is a later decision.
