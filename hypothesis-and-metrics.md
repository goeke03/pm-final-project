# Hypothesis & Success Metrics (Module 3)

## Pre-work · Hypothesis check
- **Role , who you are solving for (from M2):** A consumer in shift or variable work who cannot pay the balance in full and tries to set up an instalment plan in a short break.
- **Goal , what this user is ultimately trying to achieve:** A plan whose amount fits the month they are actually in, so they can agree to it and keep it.
- **Friction / moment of misery , the specific pain blocking their goal:** On a break, the journey asks for income and they do not know whether that means this month or a normal month, so they close it and mean to come back (N-01).
- **Current workaround , the external tool or manual process they rely on (M2):** On a break they close the journey and mean to come back once they can decide whether income means this month or a normal month (N-01). The other path in the pack is to ask a person: why_income_is_asked is 419 of 2,592 agent contacts, and on 295 of those 419 the agent repeats identity or affordability questions because the screen does not show what was already entered (N-08, T-205, T-206).
- **Problem Hook , your one-sentence framing of the business crisis (M1):** Consumers whose income changes with the shift are asked, mid-break, for a single income figure they cannot place as this month or a normal month, so they leave the plan unfinished. Fewer of them reach an agreement (41.6% versus 48.1% on a fixed salary), more of those who do miss a payment within 60 days (22.5% versus 17.4%), and the agent who inherits the case cannot see the income answers already typed.
- **Value Proposition , the outcome your initiative promised to deliver (M1):** For consumers in shift or variable work who cannot pay a balance in full, we ask for the income of the month you are in, tell you why that number is needed and how long it is kept, and if you still need a person, they already have what you entered

- **Strategic outcome , what behaviour change do you expect, and how does it map to retention / revenue / churn?:** Consumers in shift or variable work finish a plan built on the month they are in, and they are still paying 60 days later.

That is retention: agreements still paid at 60 days. Overall, 3,115 of 3,846 (81%). This segment misses 22.5% of its agreements, against 17.4% for a fixed salary. It is revenue only as the instalment that keeps coming in. The median for this segment is €63.09 a month, and the pack has no revenue total. A missed payment stops that recovery, and a spike in broken plans shows up in the client's reporting (N-10). It is churn in two places: 1,421 people leave after the affordability questions and 1,313 leave at the income document, then 731 of 3,846 agreements miss a payment (19% overall). Month 4 made the wrong trade. Agreements rose, and broken plans rose with them.
- **Primary success metric (initiative signal) , the leading indicator that tells you the gap is closing:** The agreement rate for variable-or-shift starters. The baseline is 41.6% on 2,862 starters. Fixed salary is 48.1%. It moves in the month of the journey, before the 60-day result, and it is the published gap for this consumer. Count it as the gap closing only while the guardrail holds. The overall agreement rate rose from 43.1% to 46.0% in the same months the hold got worse.
- **Guardrail metric (product signal) , the metric that must NOT drop; it protects your existing base:** The share of new agreements with no missed payment within 60 days. It must not drop. The baseline is 81.0% (3,115 of 3,846). From the miss rates, the earlier months were 84.8%, 84.1%, and 84.4% while 12 months was shown first, then 81.6%, 81.2%, and 81.0% after the 6 month plan was shown first. For variable or shift income, the current hold is 77.5%, from the 22.5% miss rate. This is the book of plans that already pay.
- **Decision window , how much time or data before you scale, pivot, or kill? minimum threshold to proceed?:** Decision window. One journey month, then 60 days. The agreement rate can be read when that month closes. The hold cannot: the guardrail is no missed payment within 60 days, so the last agreement in the cohort has to age 60 days. The earliest full decision is about 90 days from the first day of the pilot month.

The pilot segment is variable or shift income. The sample month has 2,862 of those starters. Overall starters stayed between 8,109 and 8,527, so the next month is about that size. At the 41.6% agreement rate that is about 1,190 agreements (2,862 × 0.416). That product is a calculation, not a row in the file.

Minimum to proceed. The variable-or-shift agreement rate is above 41.6%. Fixed salary is 48.1%. And the 60-day hold has not fallen below 77.5% for this segment, from the 22.5% miss rate, or below 81.0% overall (3,115 of 3,846).

Scale only if a second journey month shows the same pair. In the trend file the screen change appeared in month 4 and was still there in months 5 and 6.

Pivot if the agreement rate rises and the hold falls. That is the month-4 pattern: the agreement rate went from 44.2% to 45.6%, and the miss rate went from 15.6% to 18.4%. Do not scale the shorter plan on that result.

Kill if, after the one month plus 60 days, the agreement rate is still at or below 41.6%, or the hold has fallen. One month of about 8,300 starters was enough for this file to show a 1.4 point agreement move and a 2.8 point miss move, so this volume is enough to see a change of that size.
